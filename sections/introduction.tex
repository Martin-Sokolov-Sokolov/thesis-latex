\subsection{Related Work}

This work is inspired by Baker's seminal paper on designing PTASs in planar graphs \cite{Baker}. There have been other techniques that 
generalize this approach, namely Demaine and Hajiaghayi \cite{demaine2005bidimensionality}'s bidimensionality theory. 

\noindent The notion of Bilu-Linial stability has given rise to much exploration of structured instances in many problems. 
In their paper \cite{bilu2012stable} they offer the first evidence that stable instances are much easier than worst-case instances. 
They propose an exact algorithm for $O(n)$-stable instances of Max Cut. This result was improved by Bilu et al. \cite{bilu2012practically} 
who designed a $O(\sqrt{n})$ for the same problem. Further research yielded a general approach to analysing stable instances in 
Makarychev et al. \cite{makarychev2014bilu}. They applied that approach to graph partitioning problems and designed 
$O(\sqrt{\log n}\log\log n)$-stable instances of Max Cut and 4-stable instances of Minimum Multiway Cut. The study of perturbation 
resilience of clustering problems is explored in \cite{awasthi2012center, balcan2016clustering, balcan2015k}. On the Travelling Salesman 
Problem, Mihal\'ak et al. \cite{mihalak2011complexity} gave an algorithm for 1.8-stable instances. We refer the reader to the survey \cite{makarychev201613}.

\noindent On the subject of Beyond the Worst-Case Analysis we refer to Makarychev and Makarychev \cite{makarychev_et_al:LIPIcs.ITCS.2020.49}. 
In that paper certified algorithms are first described in an attempt to bridge the gap between the worst-case and structured instances. 
Since then, researchers have made use of the definitions and results in that paper and applied them to other problems.
Angelidakis et al. \cite{angelidakis2018bilu} explore the notion of stability and initiate the study of certified algorithms 
for the Maximum Independent Set problem (\textsc{MIS}). They provide robust and certified algorithms for $(1 + \epsilon)$-stable instances 
of planar \textsc{MIS}, for any fixed $\epsilon > 0$.
