\section{Introduction}

The \textsc{Feedback Vertex Set} (\textsc{FVS}) problem is central to the field of computer 
science. 
In Karp~\cite{Karp1972} 
it is shown that there is a reduction from \textsc{Satisfiability} to twenty-one problems, 
one of which is FVS. Because of the 
hardness of the problem, extensive research has been conducted in order to find good 
approximation solutions~\cite{bafna1995constant,bar1998approximation,becker1996optimization,chudak1998primal,goemans1998primal}. 
In this thesis, we focus on the Feedback Vertex Set with Bounded Cycle Length problem (FVS-BCL).
The goal is to find a set of vertices that intersects every cycle of a given length. 
Guruswami and Lee~\cite{guruswami2014inapproximability} prove \emph{NP-hardness} of 
approximation results, parameterized by cycle length $\rho$. More specifically, 
for any 
integer $\rho \geq 3$ and $\epsilon > 0$, it is hard to find a
$\left(\rho-1-\epsilon\right)$-approximate solution to the problem of intersecting every 
cycle of length $\rho$ in general graphs.

Another restriction 
that is imposed is on the class of graphs, namely we are only considering planar graphs. 
Two prominent approaches have led to PTASs for a variety of problems on planar and 
H-minor-free graphs. Baker's technique \cite{Baker} provides approximation algorithms for 
\textsc{NP-Complete} problems on planar graphs, while Demaine and Hajiaghayi 
\cite{demaine2005bidimensionality} extend this idea using recursive separators to apply to 
several broader classes of graphs. A well-known limitation of the shifting technique is its 
inability to handle non-local problems, such as the FVS problem.
In this thesis, we impose restrictions on 
the problems in order to introduce a local property that allows us to devise an 
EPTAS for the weighted FVS-BCL.

The \textsc{FVS} problem has applications in many different fields. 
Some of them include combinatorial circuit design~\cite{johnson1975finding}, deadlock prevention 
in computer systems~\cite{lloyd1988locating, shaw1978software}, 
VLSI chip design~\cite{gupta1989ballast,kunzmann1990analytical},
synchronous systems~\cite{luccio1998almost} 
and in artificial intelligence. More specifically, in the field of AI, the FVS problem is important 
for Bayesian inference~\cite{bar1998approximation,becker1996optimization}. 
In the context of Bayesian inference, researchers often pursue approximation algorithms for the 
FVS problem, as approximations provide a practical and effective approach.
A survey on the FVS problem and its applications by Festa et al.~\cite{festa2012feedback} gives more 
details on everything mentioned so far. The same survey argues that detecting cycles 
is an expensive operation, but ends its argument with:

\begin{quote}
The design of efficient local search procedures is also considered a key component 
in developing effective computational methods for solving the \textsc{FVS} problem.
\end{quote}

Building on this perspective, recent developments have focused on 
certified algorithms for combinatorial optimization problems~\cite{angelidakis2018bilu,bumpus_et_al:LIPIcs.SWAT.2024.19,Makarychev_Makarychev_2021}. 
In order to understand certified algorithms we first introduce 
perturbation resilience~\cite{bilu2012stable} - a condition that captures instances whose 
optimal solutions remain unchanged under small modifications to the input. 
This notion serves as a powerful foundation for designing local techniques that are 
both effective in practice and provably reliable.

\subsection*{Related Work}

The problem is \textsc{APX-complete} in general graphs
and the best approximation ratio so far is two and was first found by Becker and Geiger \cite{becker1996optimization}. Around the same time, Bafna et al. 
\cite{bafna1995constant} devised the first local ratio technique in order to arrive at the same approximation ratio. These algorithms were translated 
in primal-dual terms by Chudak et al. \cite{chudak1998primal} who constructed a third algorithm which yields a 2-approximation both for the unweighted and 
weighted case of the FVS problem. 

There has been much research on the complexity of algorithms for FVS in planar graphs. 
Kleinberg and Kumar~\cite{kleinberg2001wavelength} give the first PTAS for FVS, while 
Demaine and Hajiaghayi~\cite{demaine2005bidimensionality} give an EPTAS in the unweighted case.
Le and Zheng~\cite{le2020local} use a local search heuristic to obtain a PTAS for the 
unweighted FVS problem. For the weighted case, Cohen et al.~\cite{cohen2016approximating} give a 
PTAS for this problem in bounded-genus graphs. An interesting open problem is giving an efficient PTAS for 
the weighted case of FVS in planar graphs.

On the subject of Beyond the Worst-Case Analysis, we refer to Makarychev and Makarychev \cite{Makarychev_Makarychev_2021}. 
In that paper certified algorithms are first described in an attempt to bridge the gap between the worst-case and structured instances. 
Angelidakis et al.~\cite{angelidakis2018bilu} explore the notion of stability and initiate the study of certified algorithms 
for the \textsc{Maximum Independent Set} problem (\textsc{MIS}). They provide robust and certified algorithms for $(1 + \epsilon)$-stable instances 
of planar \textsc{MIS}, for any fixed $\epsilon > 0$. Covering and Dominating in planar graphs was further explored by Bumpus et al. \cite{bumpus_et_al:LIPIcs.SWAT.2024.19}. 
They provide ($1 + \epsilon$)-certified algorithms for \textsc{Dominating Set} and \textsc{H-Subgraph-Free-Deletion}, which for any $\epsilon$ 
run in time $f(1/\epsilon) \cdot n^{O(1)}$ on minor-closed classes of graphs of bounded local treewidth.

\subsection*{Our Contributions}

Our contributions can be grouped into three main categories.

First, we prove that both the unweighted and weighted variants of the FVS-4CL 
(which aims to break all cycles of length four) and the more general FVS-BCL problem admit 
efficient PTASs. This result is achieved through the application of Baker's technique.

Second, we present a dynamic programming algorithm for solving FVS-4CL on graphs with 
bounded treewidth.

Finally, we initiate the study of certified algorithms for both FVS-4CL and FVS-BCL. 
In the setting of planar graphs with polynomially bounded weights, we design a 
$(1+\epsilon)$-certified algorithm that runs efficiently.

\subsection*{Organization of Material}

In Section~\ref{section:preliminaries} we present every definition that is used throughout the thesis. 
In Section~\ref{section:eptas} we present 
the proof that both the unweighted and weighted FVS-4CL and FVS-BCL admit an efficient PTAS. In order 
to prove that we use a result from Section~\ref{section:exact-alg}, namely that there is an efficient algorithm for solving 
these problems in graphs of bounded treewidth. The focus is shifted to Beyond the Worst-Case analysis in 
Section~\ref{section:m-stitching}. We present the necessary lemmas to show that our problems have the structural properties, so 
we can devise a certified algorithm for them. In Section~\ref{section:certified-algs} we provide that algorithm and argue for 
the running time that is incurred.
