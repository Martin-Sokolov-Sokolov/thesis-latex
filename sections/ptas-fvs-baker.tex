\section{EPTAS for FVS-4CL and FVS-BCL using Baker's technique}
\label{section:eptas}

This section presents an EPTAS for both the unweighted and weighted versions of 
the FVS-BCL problem. We begin with the unweighted case, focusing on the algorithm's 
correctness and efficiency.

For the special case of breaking 4-cycles, denoted as FVS-4CL, exact solutions on subgraphs 
of bounded treewidth are obtained using a dynamic programming approach. In contrast, 
the general FVS-BCL problem, where cycles of arbitrary length $\rho$ must be broken, is handled using Monadic Second Order Logic (MSOL) and Courcelle’s Theorem.

The running time of the overall approach remains polynomial in the size of the input.

Some notation used throughout this section is introduced here. 
The 4-cycle-breaking problem is denoted as FVS-4CL and is used consistently in 
subsections~\ref{subsec:baker_unweighted_fvs} and~\ref{subsec:baker_weighted_fvs}, 
regardless of whether the input is weighted. In subsection~\ref{subsec:weighted_arbitrary_length}, 
where arbitrary cycle lengths are considered, the notation FVS-BCL is used. 
This distinction is maintained in subsequent chapters. Unless stated otherwise, 
FVS-4CL refers to the weighted variant of the 4-cycle-breaking problem.

\subsection{Baker's technique for unweighted FVS-4CL on planar graphs}
\label{subsec:baker_unweighted_fvs}

In this subsection we provide the necessary definitions, lemmas and theorems to demonstrate how to obtain an EPTAS for the problem 
we defined in \ref{FVS-BCL}, namely FVS-4CL. In the preliminaries we gave two examples of how to construct algorithms using Baker's technique 
and how to further analyse them. In the same fashion we now construct Algorithm \ref{alg:unweighted_feedback_vertex_set_global} and reason for its effectiveness.

 In the following algorithm we use the letter $\ell$ for the outerplanarity of each subgraph, i.e. each $G_{i, j}$ that we 
define on Line~\ref{alg3:line4} in Algorithm~\ref{alg:unweighted_feedback_vertex_set_global} is $\ell$-outerplanar.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
    \caption{Baker's technique for the unweighted FVS-4CL}
    \label{alg:unweighted_feedback_vertex_set_global}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \REQUIRE Planar graph $G = (V, E)$, parameter $\ell \leftarrow \frac{1}{\epsilon}$
    \ENSURE $(1 + \epsilon)$-approximation for \textsc{FVS-4CL}
    \STATE Perform \textsc{BFS} from some arbitrary vertex $r$ \label{alg3:line1}
    \STATE $S \leftarrow \emptyset$

    $i$: \textit{shift}; $j$: \textit{slice}

    \FOR{each $i = 0$ to $\ell-1$} \label{alg3:for-loop}
        \STATE Let $G_{i, j}$ be the subgraph induced on vertices at levels
        $j \cdot \ell + i$ through $(j+1) \cdot \ell + i + 1$ for all $j \geq 0$. \label{alg3:line4}
        \STATE Compute $S_{i, j}$, the minimum unweighted \textsc{FVS-4CL} of $G_{i, j}$ using Algorithm~\ref{alg:dp} as a subroutine (weights are all ones). \label{alg3:subroutine}
        \STATE Let $S_i = \bigcup_{j} S_{i, j}$ \label{alg3:line6}
        \STATE $S \leftarrow S \cup \{S_i\}$ \label{alg3:line7}
    \ENDFOR
    \RETURN $S_{i^*}$ from $S$ with minimum cardinality \label{alg3:line9}
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


In the analysis of the algorithm we are going to need three lemmas in order 
to prove that Algorithm~\ref{alg:unweighted_feedback_vertex_set_global} obtains a $(1 + 2/\ell)$-approximation of the 
optimum solution.

\refstepcounter{definition}
\begin{mydefinition}[Bound on the Optimum Solution in Subgraphs (Unweighted Case)]{Lemma}
    \label{lemma:opt-unweighted-fvs-4cl}
    
    Let \( S_{i,j} \) be the minimum unweighted feedback vertex set (FVS-4CL)
    for subgraph \( G_{i,j} \), computed using 
    Algorithm~\ref{alg:unweighted_feedback_vertex_set_global}, and let 
    \( F \equiv \text{OPT} \) be the optimum solution for the full graph \( G \).
    
    Define \( F_{i,j} := F \cap V(G_{i,j}) \), i.e., the restriction of the global solution \( F \) to the subgraph \( G_{i,j} \).
    
    Then:
    \[
    |S_{i,j}| \leq |F_{i,j}|
    \]
\end{mydefinition}
    

\begin{proof}
    We prove this in three steps by fixing a particular shift \( i \) and slice \( j \), 
    and analysing the corresponding subgraph \( G_{i,j} \), which is induced on the set of vertices 
    at levels from \( j \cdot \ell + i \) to \( (j + 1) \cdot \ell + i + 1 \).

    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Locality of 4-cycles:}  
        Any 4-cycle that is fully contained in \( G_{i,j} \) must be broken by vertices within \( G_{i,j} \).  
        That is, no vertex \( v \in V(G) \setminus V(G_{i,j}) \) can break 4-cycles entirely within \( G_{i,j} \).

        \item \textbf{Feasibility of restricted solution:}  
        Since \( F \) is a valid global solution for the 4-cycle-breaking problem in \( G \),  
        its restriction to \( G_{i,j} \), namely \( F_{i,j} = F \cap V(G_{i,j}) \),  
        must also be a feasible 4-cycle-breaking set for the subgraph \( G_{i,j} \).

        \item \textbf{Optimality of \( S_{i,j} \):}  
        By assumption, \( S_{i,j} \) is the minimum unweighted solution for \( G_{i,j} \).  
        Since \( F_{i,j} \) is also feasible (though not necessarily optimal), we have:
        \[
        |S_{i,j}| \leq |F_{i,j}|\text{.}
        \]
    \end{enumerate}

    This argument holds for arbitrary values of \( i \) and \( j \), so we conclude that:
    \[
    \forall i, j: \quad |S_{i,j}| \leq |F_{i,j}|\text{.}
    \]

\end{proof}

 Let $G$ look like the graph we sketched on the previous page. The vertical dots help us 
clarify the levels while the horizontal signify that there may be other vertices of interest. However, 
there are vertices we would like to inspect, specifically vertices with labels $14$ and $17$. Let 
$v_{14}, v_{17} \in F$. What follows is that $v_{14}, v_{17} \in F_{0, 0}$. The cycles that give us
reason to claim that $v_{14} \in F$ are all within $G_{0, 0}$. Therefore, it holds that
Algorithm~\ref{alg:unweighted_feedback_vertex_set_global} will have $v_{14} \in S_{0, 0}$. However, the same does not hold for 
$v_{17}$, the cycles that it is a part of are in $G_{0, 1}$ and thus $v_{17} \notin S_{0, 0}$, while 
$v_{17} \in F_{0, 0}$. This example should not distract the reader from the fact the real reason 
that $|S_{0, 0}| \leq |F_{0, 0}|$ is because $S_{0, 0}$ is by definition the minimum 4-cycle-breaking 
set for $G_{0, 0}$, while $F_{0, 0}$ is, as we previously argued, a feasible 4-cycle-breaking set.

 What follows from Lemma~\ref{lemma:opt-unweighted-fvs-4cl} is a result that holds for any shift $i$.

\refstepcounter{definition}
\begin{mydefinition}[Optimum solution bound for the whole graph (Unweighted Case)]{Result}
    \label{result-two}

    Let $S_i$ be the union of optimal solutions defined on Line~\ref{alg3:line6} of Algorithm~\ref{alg:unweighted_feedback_vertex_set_global} 
    for some shift $i$. 
    Let $F \equiv OPT$ be the optimum solution in $G$ and let $F_{i, j} = G_{i, j} \cap F$.
    For those sets it holds that: \[|S_{i}| \leq \sum\limits_{j} |S_{i, j}| \leq \sum\limits_{j} |F_{i, j}|\text{.}\]  

\end{mydefinition}

 The first inequality in Result~\ref{result-two} stems from the fact that some vertices 
in the $S_{i, j}$ sum may appear twice. This pertains to vertices that are located on the boundary. 
For example if the $v_{30} \in S_{0, 1} \wedge v_{30} \in S_{0, 2}$, then it would be counted once in 
$S_0$, but twice in $\sum\limits_j S_{0,j}$.
The second inequality is a result from Lemma~\ref{lemma:opt-unweighted-fvs-4cl} 
where we argue that $S_{i, j}$ is an optimum solution for $G_{i, j}$ while $F_{i, j}$ is a 
4-cycle-breaking set, but not one of necessarily minimum cardinality. 

 In Lemma~\ref{lemma-three} we look at the bound of the union of the $F_{i, j}$ sets. 
Before that, however, we make an argument for the vertices on the boundaries in Lemma~\ref{lemma-two}. 

\refstepcounter{definition}
\begin{mydefinition}[Bound for the vertices on the boundaries (Unweighted Case)]{Lemma}
    \label{lemma-two}

    Let $F_i = F$ $\cap$ \{vertices at levels $i$ mod $\ell\}$. 
    The sets $F_i$ are disjoint and $\bigcup_i F_i = F$.

    We claim that: \[\exists q \in \{0, 1, \dots, \ell-1\}:
     |F_q| + |F_{q+1}| \leq \frac{2}{\ell} \cdot |F| \text{.}\]

\end{mydefinition}

\begin{proof}
    Assume for the sake of contradiction that
    \begin{equation}
        \forall i\in\{0,1,\dots,\ell-1\}:\quad
        |F_i| + |F_{i+1}| \;>\; \frac{2}{\ell}\,|F|.
        \label{ineq:opt_bound}
    \end{equation}
    Then we argue in three steps:
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item Summing \ref{ineq:opt_bound} over all \(i=0,\dots,\ell-1\) gives
        \[
          \sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\bigl(|F_i|+|F_{i+1}|\bigr)
          \;>\;
          \sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\frac{2}{\ell}\,|F|
          \;=\;
          2\,|F|.
        \]
      \item On the other hand, since the sets \(F_0,\dots,F_{\ell-1}\) are pairwise disjoint
        and \(\bigcup_iF_i=F\), each \(F_i\) appears exactly twice in the left-hand sum.  Therefore
        \[
          \sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\bigl(|F_i|+|F_{i+1}|\bigr)
          =2\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}|F_i|
          =2\,|F|,
        \]
        yielding a contradiction.
      \item Hence the assumption \(\forall i:\;|F_i|+|F_{i+1}|>\tfrac{2}{\ell}|F|\) must be false.  By the pigeonhole principle there is at least one index 
        \[
          q\in\{0,1,\dots,\ell-1\}
          \quad\text{such that}\quad
          |F_q| + |F_{q+1}|\;\le\;\frac{2}{\ell}\,|F|.
        \]
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proof}
    
Now we argue for the size of the sets $F_{i, j}$. It is natural to try and 
tie their size to the size of the optimal solution $F$ using Lemma~\ref{lemma-two}. 
Whenever we refer to $F_i$ and $F_{i+1}$,
one should keep in mind that the second term may wrap around and become $F_0$.

\refstepcounter{definition}
\begin{mydefinition}[Bound for the cardinality of the intersection sets (Unweighted Case)]{Lemma}
    \label{lemma-three}

    Let $F \equiv OPT$ be the optimal solution and $F_{i, j} = F \cap G_{i, j}$ be the intersection 
    sets with the subgraphs. Then we have: 
    \[\sum\limits_{j} |F_{i^*, j}| \leq |F| + \frac{2}{\ell} \cdot |F|\]

    for some specific integer $i^*$.

\end{mydefinition}

\begin{proof}
    
If we look at Fig.~\ref{fig:bfs_tree}, then we notice that any vertex that is located 
in the overlap layers would be counted twice in the sum $\sum\limits_{j} |F_{i, j}|$. The interior 
vertices are counted once towards the optimum solution sum. Therefore, we have that: 

\begin{enumerate}
    \item $\sum\limits_{j} |F_{i, j}| = |F| + |\{\text{vertices on the boundary}\}| = |F| + |F_i| + |F_{i+1}|$
    for $i \in \{0, 1, \dots, \ell-1\}$. \label{enum:eq1}
    \item For the RHS of the equation in step~\ref{enum:eq1}. we argue that:
    \[\exists i^*: |F_{i^*}| + |F_{i^*+1}| \leq \frac{2}{\ell} \cdot |F|\] using Lemma~\ref{lemma-two}.
    \item For that $i^*$ it holds that $\sum\limits_{j} |F_{i^*, j}| \leq |F| + \frac{2}{\ell} |F|$.
\end{enumerate}

\end{proof}

Finally, we chain the inequalities presented in Lemma~\ref{lemma:opt-unweighted-fvs-4cl}, Result~\ref{result-two}
and Lemma~\ref{lemma-three}, and get that for some slice $i^*$, it holds that:

\[|S_{i^*}| \leq \sum\limits_{j} |S_{i^*, j}| \leq \sum\limits_{j} |F_{i^*, j}| \leq |F| + \frac{2}{\ell} \cdot |F| = (1 + \frac{2}{\ell}) \cdot |F|\].

The inequality
\[
|S_{i^*}| \leq \left(1 + \frac{2}{\ell} \right) \cdot |F|
\]
implies that Algorithm~\ref{alg:unweighted_feedback_vertex_set_global} returns a 
\(\left(1 + \frac{2}{\ell}\right)\)-approximate solution. Since we set \(\ell \leftarrow \frac{1}{\epsilon}\), 
this yields a \((1 + 2\epsilon)\)-approximation for the unweighted planar FVS-4CL problem.


\textbf{Running time:}

Lines~\ref{alg3:line1} and~\ref{alg3:line9} of Algorithm~\ref{alg:unweighted_feedback_vertex_set_global} 
can be computed in linear time $O(n)$. The loop that starts on Line~\ref{alg3:for-loop} has a running time
that is dominated by step~\ref{alg3:subroutine} where we call Algorithm~\ref{alg:dp} (with weights equal to ones) as a subroutine.
That algorithm takes as input subgraphs that are $\ell + 2$-outerplanar.
As we argued in the Preliminaries section, such graphs have bounded treewidth of $\tw$,
Bodlaender~\cite[Theorem 83]{bodlaender1998partial}.
Therefore, Algorithm~\ref{alg:unweighted_feedback_vertex_set_global} has a running time of 
$2^{\mathcal{O}(\tw^2)} \cdot n$.

\subsection{Baker's technique for the weighted FVS-4CL on planar graphs}
\label{subsec:baker_weighted_fvs}

In this subsection we look at the weighted version of the problem. 
Firstly, we construct Algorithm~\ref{alg:weighted_feedback_vertex_set_global} which differs from Algorithm~\ref{alg:unweighted_feedback_vertex_set_global} 
only on step~\ref{alg4:step5}. On Line~\ref{alg4:step5}
we construct Algorithm~\ref{courcelle:weighted} and use it as a subroutine to get the solution we want.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
    \caption{Baker's technique for the weighted \textsc{FVS-4CL}}
    \label{alg:weighted_feedback_vertex_set_global}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \REQUIRE Planar graph $G = (V, E)$, vertex weight function $w : V \rightarrow \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$, parameter $\ell \leftarrow \frac{1}{\epsilon}$
    \ENSURE $(1 + \epsilon)$-approximation for \textsc{FVS-4CL}
    \STATE Perform \textsc{BFS} from some arbitrary root vertex $r$
    \STATE $S \leftarrow \emptyset$
    
    $i$: \textit{shift}; $j$: \textit{slice}
    
    \FOR{each $i = 0$ to $\ell - 1$}
        \STATE Let $G_{i,j}$ be the subgraph induced on vertices at levels $j \cdot \ell + i$ through $(j+1) \cdot \ell + i + 1$, for all $j \geq 0$
        \STATE Let $S_{i, j}$ be the exact solution of the weighted FVS-4CL on the subgraph $G_{i, j}$ using Algorithm~\ref{alg:dp} as a subroutine \label{alg4:step5}
        \STATE Let $S_i = \bigcup_{j} S_{i,j}$
        \STATE $S \leftarrow S \cup \{S_i\}$
    \ENDFOR
    \RETURN $S_{i^*} \in S$ such that $w(S_{i^*}) \leq w(S_i)$ for all $i \in \{0, 1, \dots, \ell - 1\}$
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


Much like the proof we did in subsection~\ref{subsec:baker_unweighted_fvs}, we start off with 
finding a bound for the optimum solution for each subgraph. 

\refstepcounter{definition}
\begin{mydefinition}[Optimum solution bound (Weighted Case)]{Lemma}
    \label{lemma-osbwc}

    Let $G = (V, E)$ be a planar graph and $w : V \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be a weight function.
    Let $S_{i, j}$ be an optimum solution for $G_{i, j}$ for some shift $i$ and slice $j$, 
    let $F \equiv OPT$ be the optimum solution in $G$ and let $F_{i, j} = G_{i, j} \cap F$. 
    In the weighted case we claim that: \[w(S_{i, j}) \leq w(F_{i, j})\text{.}\]

\end{mydefinition}

\begin{proof}
    
    We prove this in three steps by fixing a particular shift $i$ and slice $j$, 
    and analysing the corresponding subgraph $G_{i, j}$, which is induced on the set of vertices 
    at levels from $j \cdot \ell + i$ to $(j + 1) \cdot \ell + i + 1$.
    
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Locality of 4-cycles:}  
        Any 4-cycle that is fully contained in $G_{i, j}$ must be broken by vertices within $G_{i, j}$.  
        That is, no vertex \( v \in V(G) \setminus V(G_{i, j}) \) can break 4-cycles entirely within \( G_{i, j} \).
    
        \item \textbf{Feasibility of restricted solution:}  
        Since $F$ is a valid global solution for the 4-cycle-breaking problem in $G$,  
        its restriction to $G_{i, j}$, namely \( F_{i, j} = F \cap G_{i, j} \),  
        must also be a feasible 4-cycle-breaking set for the subgraph $G_{i, j}$.
    
        \item \textbf{Optimality of $S_{i, j}$:}  
        By assumption, $S_{i, j}$ is the minimum-weight solution for $G_{i, j}$.  
        Since $F_{i, j}$ is also feasible (though not necessarily optimal), we have:
        \[
        w(S_{i, j}) \leq w(F_{i, j})\text{.}
        \]
    \end{enumerate}
    
    This argument holds for arbitrary values of \( i \) and \( j \), so we conclude that:
    \[
    \forall i, j: \quad w(S_{i, j}) \leq w(F_{i, j})\text{.}
    \]
    
\end{proof}

\refstepcounter{definition}
\begin{mydefinition}[Optimum solution bound for the whole graph (Weighted Case)]{Result}
    \label{result-osbwc}

    Let $S_i$ be the union of optimal solutions defined on Line 6 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:weighted_feedback_vertex_set_global} 
    for some shift $i$.
    Let $F \equiv OPT$ be the optimum solution in $G$ and let $F_{i, j} = G_{i, j} \cap F$.
    For those sets it holds that: \[w(S_{i}) \leq \sum\limits_{j} w(S_{i, j}) \leq \sum\limits_{j} w(F_{i, j}) \text{.}\]  

\end{mydefinition}


\begin{proof}
    
This result immediately follows from the fact that when we define $S_i$ as the union of the subgraphs 
some vertices, specifically those on the overlapping layers, will have their weights added twice 
to the sum $\sum\limits_{j} w(S_{i, j})$. If we inspect the LHS, however, each vertex and its weight will be 
counted only once towards $w(S_i)$. Therefore, it holds that $w(S_i) \leq \sum\limits_{j} w(S_{i, j})$ 
and from Lemma~\ref{lemma-osbwc} we chain the inequalities to get Result~\ref{result-osbwc}.

\end{proof}

\refstepcounter{definition}
\begin{mydefinition}[Bound on the Weight of Boundary Vertices (Weighted Case)]{Lemma}
\label{lemma-bvweighted_feedback_vertex_set_global}

Let \( G = (V, E) \) be a planar graph, and let \( w : V \rightarrow \mathbb{R} \) be a weight function on the vertices. 

Let \( F \subseteq V \) be a feedback vertex set, and define the sets \( F_i \) as:
\[
F_i := F \cap \{ \text{vertices at BFS level } i \bmod \ell \}
\]
for each \( i \in \{0, 1, \dots, \ell - 1\} \). Then, the sets \( F_i \) are disjoint, and:
\[
F = \bigcup_{i = 0}^{\ell - 1} F_i
\]

We claim that:
\[
\exists q \in \{0, 1, \dots, \ell - 1\} \text{ such that } w(F_q) + w(F_{q+1}) \leq \frac{2}{\ell} \cdot w(F)
\]
\end{mydefinition}

\begin{proof}
Assume by way of contradiction that the following inequality holds for all \( i \in \{0, 1, \dots, \ell - 1\} \) (with indices taken modulo \( \ell \)):
\begin{equation}
    w(F_i) + w(F_{i+1}) > \frac{2}{\ell} \cdot w(F)
    \label{ineq:two}
\end{equation}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item From the definitions of the sets \( F_i \), we know:
    \[F_i \cap F_j = \emptyset \quad \text{for all } i \neq j\]
    \[F = \bigcup_{i = 0}^{\ell - 1} F_i\]
    Therefore, each vertex in \( F \) is in exactly one \( F_i \), and:
    \[
        \sum_{i=0}^{\ell - 1} w(F_i) = w(F)
    \]

    When summing all pairs \( w(F_i) + w(F_{i+1}) \), each term \( w(F_i) \) appears exactly twice (once in the pair \( (F_{i-1}, F_i) \) and once in \( (F_i, F_{i+1}) \)). So:
    \[
        \sum_{i=0}^{\ell - 1} \bigl( w(F_i) + w(F_{i+1}) \bigr) = 2 \cdot w(F)
    \]

    \item Now, under the assumption of inequality~\eqref{ineq:two}, we would have:
    \[
        \sum_{i=0}^{\ell - 1} \bigl( w(F_i) + w(F_{i+1}) \bigr) > \sum_{i=0}^{\ell - 1} \frac{2}{\ell} \cdot w(F) = 2 \cdot w(F)
    \]
    which contradicts the conclusion from step 1 that this sum equals \( 2 \cdot w(F) \).

    \item Therefore, our assumption must be false. By the contrapositive, there must exist at least one index \( q \in \{0, 1, \dots, \ell - 1\} \) such that:
    \[
        w(F_q) + w(F_{q+1}) \leq \frac{2}{\ell} \cdot w(F)
    \]
    This completes the proof.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\refstepcounter{definition}
\begin{mydefinition}[Bound for the weight of the intersection sets (Weighted Case)]{Lemma}
    \label{lemma-bcisweighted_feedback_vertex_set_global}

    Let $F \equiv OPT$ be the optimal solution of the FVS-4CL problem.
    Let $F_{i, j} = F \cap G_{i, j}$ be the intersection 
    sets with the subgraphs. Then we have: 
    \[\sum\limits_{j} w(F_{i^{*}, j}) \leq w(F) + \frac{2}{\ell} \cdot w(F)\]

    for some specific integer $i^{*}$.

\end{mydefinition}

\begin{proof}
    
    The interior vertices of each subgraph have their weights counted only once towards the sum 
    $\sum\limits_j w(F_{i, j})$. The same does not hold for the vertices on the boundary, they are counted 
    once in $F_{i, j}$ and once in $F_{i, j+1}$. To account for that we rewrite the sum on the LHS as:

    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $\sum\limits_j w(F_{i, j}) = w(F) + w(\{\text{vertices on the boundary}\})$.
        \item From Lemma~\ref{lemma-bvweighted_feedback_vertex_set_global} we have an integer $i^* \in \{1, 2, \dots, \ell-1\}$, such that:
        
        $w(F_{i^*}) + w(F_{i^* + 1}) \leq \frac{2}{\ell} \cdot w(F)$.
        \item From the previous two steps we get:
        \[\sum\limits_j w(F_{i, j}) =
        w(F) + w(\{\text{vertices on the boundary}\}) \leq
        \frac{2}{\ell} \cdot w(F)
        \]
    \end{enumerate}

\end{proof}

 Finally, we chain the inequalities we obtained in Lemma~\ref{lemma-osbwc}, Result~\ref{result-osbwc}, 
and Lemma~\ref{lemma-bcisweighted_feedback_vertex_set_global} to argue that for some $i^* \in \{1, 2, \dots, \ell-1\}$: 

\[w(S_{i^*}) \leq \sum\limits_j w(S_{i^*, j}) \leq \sum\limits_j w(F_{i^*, j}) \leq w(F) + \frac{2}{\ell} \cdot w(F)\].

 \textbf{Running time:}

The only difference from the unweighted case comes from Line~\ref{alg4:step5} of 
Algorithm~\ref{alg:weighted_feedback_vertex_set_global}. However, using Algorithm~\ref{alg:dp} 
we get that the running time of the algorithm is still $2^{\mathcal{O}(\tw^2)} \cdot n$.

\subsection{Baker's technique for weighted FVS-BCL of arbitrary cycle length}
\label{subsec:weighted_arbitrary_length}

This subsection extends the concepts discussed in subsection~\ref{subsec:baker_weighted_fvs} 
by examining the outcome of attempting to break all weighted cycles of length $\rho$. 

\begin{algorithm}[H]
    \caption{Baker's technique for the weighted \textsc{FVS-BCL}}
    \label{alg:baker_arbitrary_length}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \REQUIRE Planar graph $G = (V, E)$, vertex weight function $w : V \rightarrow \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$, parameter $\ell \leftarrow \frac{1}{\epsilon}$, integer $\rho$ specifying the maximum cycle length to break
    \ENSURE $(1 + \lfloor \frac{\rho}{2} \rfloor \cdot \epsilon)$-approximation for \textsc{FVS-BCL}
    \STATE Perform \textsc{BFS} from some arbitrary root vertex $r$ \label{alg5:line1}
    \STATE $S \leftarrow \emptyset$
    
    $i$: \textit{shift}; $j$: \textit{slice}
    
    \FOR{each $i = 0$ to $\ell - 1$}
        \STATE Let $G_{i,j}$ be the subgraph induced on vertices at levels $j \cdot \ell + i$ through $(j+1) \cdot \ell + i + \left\lfloor \frac{\rho}{2} \right\rfloor$, for all $j \geq 0$ \label{alg5:line4}
        \STATE Let $S_{i, j}$ be the exact solution of the weighted FVS-BCL on the subgraph $G_{i, j}$ using Corollary~\ref{cor:msol-fvs-rho} \label{alg5:line5}
        \STATE Let $S_i = \bigcup_{j} S_{i,j}$ \label{alg5:line6}
        \STATE $S \leftarrow S \cup \{S_i\}$ \label{alg5:line7}
    \ENDFOR
    \RETURN $S_{i^*} \in S$ such that $w(S_{i^*}) \leq w(S_i)$ for all $i \in \{0, 1, \dots, \ell - 1\}$ \label{alg5:line9}
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


 The changes between Algorithm~\ref{alg:weighted_feedback_vertex_set_global} and 
Algorithm~\ref{alg:baker_arbitrary_length} pertain to Lines \ref{alg5:line4}-\ref{alg5:line7}.
The main difference is on Line~\ref{alg5:line4} where 
we define the subgraphs we want to inspect. This change enables us to argue that the union 
of the local solutions, defined on Line~\ref{alg5:line6}, is indeed a feasible solution for the
whole graph. 

\refstepcounter{definition}
\begin{mydefinition}[Optimum solution bound for FVS-$\rho$CL]{Lemma}
    \label{lemma:optimum_solution_bound_rho_cycle_length}

    Let $G = (V, E)$ be a planar graph, $w : V \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be a weight function and 
    $\rho$ an integer. Let $F \equiv OPT$ be the optimum solution in the whole graph for 
    FVS-$\rho$-BCL and $F_{i, j} = G_{i, j} \cap F$.
    Let $S_{i, j}$ be an optimum solution for $G_{i, j}$ for some shift $i$ and slice $j$.
    We claim that: \[w(S_i) \leq \sum\limits_{j} w(S_{i, j}) \leq \sum\limits_{j} w(F_{i, j})\text{.}\]

\end{mydefinition}

\begin{proof}
    We prove this in three steps by fixing a particular shift \( i \) and analysing the solution 
    constructed from slices \( G_{i,j} \), each induced on vertices at levels 
    from \( j \cdot \ell + i \) to \( (j + 1) \cdot \ell + i + \left\lfloor \frac{\rho}{2} \right\rfloor \), 
    where \( \rho \) is the maximum cycle length we wish to break.

    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Locality of \(\rho\)-cycles:}  
        Any cycle of length \( \rho \) that is entirely contained within \( G_{i,j} \) 
        must be broken by vertices from within \( G_{i,j} \).  
        That is, no vertex \( v \in V(G) \setminus V(G_{i,j}) \) can break such cycles in \( G_{i,j} \).

        \item \textbf{Feasibility of restricted solution:}  
        Since \( F \) is a valid global solution that breaks all cycles of length \( \rho \),  
        the restriction \( F_{i,j} = F \cap V(G_{i,j}) \) is a feasible solution for the 
        \(\rho\)-cycle-breaking problem in \( G_{i,j} \).  
        By definition, \( S_{i,j} \) is the optimal solution for \( G_{i,j} \), so:
        \[
        w(S_{i,j}) \leq w(F_{i,j})
        \]
        Summing over all \( j \), we get:
        \[
        \sum_j w(S_{i,j}) \leq \sum_j w(F_{i,j})
        \]

        \item \textbf{Overlap and accounting for duplicates:}  
        The total weight \( w(S_i) \) of the combined solution across slices may be smaller than 
        \( \sum_j w(S_{i,j}) \), because vertices near slice boundaries can appear in multiple \( G_{i,j} \)  
        but are only counted once in \( S_i \). Therefore:
        \[
        w(S_i) \leq \sum_j w(S_{i,j})
        \]
    \end{enumerate}

    Chaining both inequalities, we conclude:
    \[
    w(S_i) \leq \sum_j w(S_{i,j}) \leq \sum_j w(F_{i,j})\text{.}
    \]
\end{proof}

\begin{mydefinition}[Bound on the Weight of Boundary Vertices for FVS-$\rho$CL]{Lemma}
    \label{lemma:boundary_weight_bound_rho}
    
    Let \( G = (V, E) \) be a planar graph, and let \( w : V \rightarrow \mathbb{R} \) be a weight function on the vertices.  
    Let \( F \subseteq V \) be a solution to the FVS-\(\rho\)CL problem (i.e., a set that breaks all cycles of length \( \rho \)).  
    For a fixed layering from a BFS traversal, define:
    \[
    F_i := F \cap \{ \text{vertices at level } i \bmod \ell \}
    \]
    for each \( i \in \{0, 1, \ldots, \ell - 1\} \). Then the sets \( F_i \) are disjoint and together cover all of \( F \):
    \[
    F = \bigcup_{i=0}^{\ell - 1} F_i\text{.}
    \]
    
    We claim that there exists some \( q \in \{0, 1, \ldots, \ell - 1\} \) such that:
    \[
    w(F_q) + w(F_{q+1}) + \cdots + w(F_{q + \lfloor \rho/2 \rfloor - 1}) \leq \frac{\lfloor \rho/2 \rfloor}{\ell} \cdot w(F)
    \]
    where the indices are taken modulo \( \ell \).
\end{mydefinition}

\begin{proof}
    Assume by way of contradiction that the above inequality does not hold for any \( i \in \{0, 1, \ldots, \ell - 1\} \).  
    That is, suppose:
    \[
    \forall i: \quad w(F_i) + w(F_{i+1}) + \cdots + w(F_{i + \lfloor \rho/2 \rfloor - 1}) > \frac{\lfloor \rho/2 \rfloor}{\ell} \cdot w(F)
    \]
    
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item From the definition of the sets \( F_i \), we know that:
        \[
        F_i \cap F_j = \emptyset \quad \text{for all } i \neq j, \quad \text{and} \quad \bigcup_{i=0}^{\ell - 1} F_i = F
        \]
        Hence,
        \[
        \sum_{i=0}^{\ell - 1} w(F_i) = w(F)
        \]

        \item \textbf{Counting each vertex \(\lfloor \rho/2 \rfloor\) times:}  
        When we sum the weights of all such consecutive windows of length \( \lfloor \rho/2 \rfloor \) across \( \ell \) values of \( i \),  
        each set \( F_j \) is included in exactly \( \lfloor \rho/2 \rfloor \) of these sums (i.e., it appears in that many overlapping windows).
        Therefore:
        \[
        \sum_{i=0}^{\ell - 1} \left( w(F_i) + \cdots + w(F_{i + \lfloor \rho/2 \rfloor - 1}) \right) = \lfloor \rho/2 \rfloor \cdot w(F)
        \]

        \item \textbf{Contradiction:}  
        On the other hand, if every window sum is strictly greater than \( \frac{\lfloor \rho/2 \rfloor}{\ell} \cdot w(F) \), then:
        \[
        \sum_{i=0}^{\ell - 1} \left( w(F_i) + \cdots + w(F_{i + \lfloor \rho/2 \rfloor - 1}) \right)
        > \ell \cdot \frac{\lfloor \rho/2 \rfloor}{\ell} \cdot w(F)
        = \lfloor \rho/2 \rfloor \cdot w(F)
        \]
        which contradicts the equality from step 2.

        \item \textbf{Conclusion:}  
        Therefore, our assumption must be false. We conclude that:
        \[
        \exists q \in \{0, 1, \ldots, \ell - 1\} \text{ such that }
        w(F_q) + \cdots + w(F_{q + \lfloor \rho/2 \rfloor - 1}) \leq \frac{\lfloor \rho/2 \rfloor}{\ell} \cdot w(F)
        \]
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proof}


\refstepcounter{definition}
\begin{mydefinition}[Bound for the weight of the intersection sets (Weighted Case)]{Lemma}
    \label{lemma-rho-weighted-bound}

    Let \( F \equiv \text{OPT} \) be the optimal solution to the FVS-\(\rho\)CL problem for some \(\rho \in \mathbb{N}_{\geq 3}\),
    and let \( F_{i, j} = F \cap G_{i, j} \) be the intersection of \( F \) with the subgraphs. Then there exists an
    integer \( i^* \in \{0, 1, \dots, \ell - 1\} \) such that:
    \[
        \sum\limits_{j} w(F_{i^*, j}) \leq w(F) + \frac{\lfloor \rho/2 \rfloor}{\ell} \cdot w(F)
    \]
\end{mydefinition}

\begin{proof}

When observing the construction of the subgraphs \( G_{i, j} \), we note that the vertices within the overlap regions
(boundary layers) are counted $\lfloor \rho / 2 \rfloor$ in the total sum \( \sum\limits_{j} w(F_{i, j}) \). The remaining (interior)
vertices of each \( G_{i, j} \) contribute exactly once to the total weight. This gives us:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Vertices on the boundary: \[
        \sum\limits_{j} w(F_{i, j}) = w(F) + w(\{\text{vertices on the boundary}\}) = 
        w(F) + w(F_i) + w(F_{i+1}) + \dots + w(F_{i+\lfloor \rho/2 \rfloor - 1})
    \]
    for some \( i \in \{0, 1, \dots, \ell - 1\} \). \label{enum:rho-eq1}
    
    \item From Lemma~\ref{lemma:boundary_weight_bound_rho}, we know there exists some \( i^* \in \{0, 1, \dots, \ell - 1\} \) such that:
    \[
        w(F_{i^*}) + w(F_{i^* + 1}) + \dots + w(F_{i^* + \lfloor \rho/2 \rfloor - 1}) \leq \frac{\lfloor \rho/2 \rfloor}{\ell} \cdot w(F)
    \]
    
    \item Plugging this into step~\ref{enum:rho-eq1}, we obtain:
    \[
        \sum\limits_{j} w(F_{i^*, j}) \leq w(F) + \frac{\lfloor \rho/2 \rfloor}{\ell} \cdot w(F)
    \]
\end{enumerate}

\end{proof}


\textbf{Running time:}

In Algorithm~\ref{alg:baker_arbitrary_length} which solves FVS-BCL the computation on Line~\ref{alg5:line5}
dominates the running time. In planar graphs steps \ref{alg5:line1} and \ref{alg5:line9} take linear time 
to the input. Using Courcelle's theorem \cite{courcelle1990monadic} we argue for the running time 
of Line~\ref{alg5:line5}, which uses Corollary.~\ref{cor:msol-fvs-rho} as a result. As we will say in the next 
section, the running time is $f(\rho, t) \cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}$ for some computable function $f$ 
where a tree decomposition of width $t$ is provided. Using a result from Bodlaender~\cite[Theorem 83]{bodlaender1998partial}
one can incur that the treewidth of each subgraph is bounded by $\tw$. Therefore the running time of Alg.~\ref{alg:baker_arbitrary_length} 
is $\mathcal{O}(f(\rho, \tw) \cdot n)$ for some computable function $f$.
