\section{Research Question}

In this work, we explore several questions. The first two are of most significance, while the problems that follow 
will be tackled in what time is left.

\begin{itemize}[left=0pt, labelsep=5pt]
    \item Explore the \textsc{FVS-BCL} problem as defined in Definition  in planar graphs.
    \begin{itemize}[left=2pt, labelsep=5pt]
        \item To that end we are going to make use of Baker's shifting technique \cite{Baker} as presented in Algorithm~\ref{alg:baker}.
        \item By using the modified version with an overlap between layers of $\left\lfloor \frac{r}{2} \right\rfloor + 1$,
        we capture the essence of breaking cycles of bounded length $r$.
        \item Baker's technique is known to work for problems that involve local conditions on nodes and edges and our imposed restrictions on the \textsc{FVS}
        allow us to deploy this framework.
        \item We extend this method to devise a PTAS for the weighted version of the problem, \textsc{MWFVS-BCL} for planar graphs.
    \end{itemize}
    \item Initiate the study of certified algorithms for \textsc{MWFVS-BCL}.
    We provide robust and certified algorithms for $(1 + \epsilon)$-stable instances of planar \textsc{MWFVS-BCL}, for any
    fixed $\epsilon > 0$. 
    \item Future Directions:
    \begin{itemize}[left=2pt, labelsep=5pt]
        \item Investigating the \textsc{Cycle Packing-BCL} problem. As a hereditary maximization task, we make use of the
        alternative approach to Baker's technique, namely Algorithm~\ref{alg:bakeremoval} with removal.
        \item Exploring the translation of PTASs for the \textsc{TSP} to $(1 + \epsilon)$-certified algorithms.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\noindent Firstly, we are going to explore the problem of \textsc{FVS} in planar graphs where we try to break all cycles of bounded length $r$. 
To that end we are going to make use of Baker's shifting technique \cite{Baker}. The reader can imagine a modified version of Algorithm~\ref{alg:baker}
where 
the overlap between layers is $\left\lfloor \frac{r}{2} \right\rfloor + 1$. On an intuitive level, this number captures the fact that each 
(bounded) cycle, that has to broken, starting from a node in the \textsc{BFS} tree \textit{has to go back to the starting vertex}. 
Baker's technique is known to work for problems that involve local conditions on nodes and edges and our imposed restrictions on the \textsc{FVS}
allow us to deploy this framework. Then we move on to the weighted version of the problem. For the same conditions we devise a PTAS for the 
\textsc{MWFVS} problem. 

\noindent Secondly, we initiate the study of certified algorithms for \textsc{MWFVS}. More precisely, we provide robust and 
certified algorithms for $(1 + \epsilon)$-stable instances of planar \textsc{MWFVS} with cycles of bounded length, 
for any fixed $\epsilon > 0$. 

\noindent The authors plan to continue this area of research if time allows it, by exploring the following avenues. 

\noindent The first one is looking at the \textsc{Cycle Packing} problem. In the Preliminaries section we described hereditary maximization tasks. 
This problem falls into that category, thus allowing us to make use of the alternative approach to Baker's technique, 
namely Algorithm~\ref{alg:bakeremoval} with removal.

\noindent The second one involves checking if we can translate current PTASs for the \textsc{TSP} to $(1 + \epsilon)$-certified algorithms.
